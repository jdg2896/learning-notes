# Writing Lessons

## Beginner vs Expert Writer

- Beginners have the gift of noobness
  - Their documented experiences can relate to more readers who are also noobs
- Experts have the curse of knowledge
  - Experts might miss the perspective of noobs learning for the first time

## Writer Interview - Robin Wieruch

- Set some time in the morning to write before the real work.
- Write about things that interest you, so it doesn't feel like a chore.
- Don't force yourself if you don't like writing.
  - I like writing, and it's one of the few channels where I can really express myself as an introvert.
- Be consistent in writing
  - Take breaks when needed, but don't slack off too much

## My Lessons from Writing Weekly Articles

After a ~9 month hiatus from writing weekly articles:

1. It's okay to be motivated by a negative experience, but it's not okay if that is your only motivation. It's weak and will fizzle out. You need intrinsic motivation (a passion for learning). If you're already writing notes on what you learn or experience, sharing it publicly is only one added step. Writing is still one of the better means of communication: you can freely express your thoughts, and it's effective at sharing knowledge.
2. Avoid relying on social media. My target audience isn't really my IRL connections, but whoever it reaches. The dopamine hits from social media don't help either, so I'd rather write without pressure and build a readerbase organically.
3. Set up a personal blog, and don't get too hung up on "challenges". Weekly challenges didn't work for me. A routine is more manageable (e.g. 30 minutes per day, with discipline). Write at your own pace about stuff that interests you.
