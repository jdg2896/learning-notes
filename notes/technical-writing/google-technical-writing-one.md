# Google Technical Writing One

[Course link](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one)

## Key Takeaways

- Use terms consistently.
- Avoid ambiguous pronouns.
- Prefer active voice to passive voice.
- Pick specific verbs over vague ones.
- Focus each sentence on a single idea.
- Convert some long sentences to lists.
- Eliminate unneeded words.
- Use a numbered list when ordering is important and a bulleted list when ordering is irrelevant.
- Keep list items parallel.
- Start numbered list items with imperative words.
- Introduce lists and tables appropriately.
- Create great opening sentences that establish a paragraph's central point.
- Focus each paragraph on a single topic.
- Determine what your audience needs to learn.
- Fit documentation to your audience.
- Establish your document's key points at the start of the document.

## Words

- Define new or unfamiliar terms
- Use terms consistently
- Use acronyms properly
- Recognize ambiguous pronouns

## Active Voice

- Recognize passive verbs
  - past participle verbs
- Imperative verbs are typically active
- Prefer active voice to passive voice

## Clear Sentences

- Choose strong verbs
- Reduce "there is / there are"
- Minimize certain adjectives and adverbs
  - don't make it sound like marketing (example: screamingly fast)

## Short Sentences

- Focus each sentence on a single idea
- Convert some long sentences to lists
- Eliminate unneeded words
- Reduce subordinate clauses
  - Distinguish _that_ from _which_

## Lists and Tables

- Choose the correct type of list
  - bulleted (unordered)
  - numbered (ordered)
  - embedded
- Keep list items parallel
- Start numbered list items with imperative verbs
- Punctuate items appropriately
- Create useful tables
- Introduce each list and table

## Paragraphs

- **One topic** per paragraph
- Focus on a **great opening sentence**; succeeding sentences should support it
- Paragraphs shouldn't be too long, **3-5 sentences** each
- Paragraphs shouldn't be too short; combine one-line sentences into a more cohesive group

Good paragraphs answer the following three questions:

1. **What** are you trying to tell your reader?
2. **Why** is it important for the reader to know this?
3. **How** should the reader use this knowledge? Alternatively, how should the reader know your point to be true?

## Audience

- Define your audience's role
  - Software engineers
  - Technical, non-engineer roles
  - Students
  - Non-technical roles
- Determine what your audience needs to learn
- Fit documentation to your audience
  - Vocabulary, explain more
  - Curse of knowledge
  - Simple words
  - Cultural neutrality and idioms
    - "a piece of cake" (idiom)

## Documents

- State your document's scope
  - also state the non-scope
- State your audience
- State any prerequisite knowledge for the article
- Summarize key points at the start
- Compare and contrast
- Write for your audience
  - Who is your target audience?
  - What is your target audience's goal? Why are they reading this document?
  - What do your readers already know _before_ they read your document?
  - What should your readers know or be able to do _after_ they read your document?
- Organize the document to meet your audience's needs

## Punctuation

- Commas
  - use commas at natural pauses
  - use commas to separate items
  - don't use commas to separate two sentences
- Semicolons
  - A period separates distinct thoughts; a semicolon unites highly related thoughts.
- Em dashes
- En dashes
  - Don't use
- Parentheses
  - hold minor points
  - keep usage to a minimum
